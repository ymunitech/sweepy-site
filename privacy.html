Sweepy: Photo & Storage Cleaner for iPhone · Last updated: November 2, 2026

The short version

Sweepy is a storage cleaner for iPhone. It finds duplicate and similar photos, screenshots, blurry shots, Live Photos and large videos, helps you tidy your contacts, and shows a health score for your device. Everything Sweepy does with your content happens on your iPhone. Sweepy has no account, no servers that receive your photos, contacts or messages, no advertising SDKs, and it does not sell personal data. The only things that may leave your phone are anonymous usage statistics (which screens and features are used, how many items were cleaned) sent to our analytics provider, Amplitude, and whatever you choose to send yourself (for example, a photo you share or an email to support).

2. What Sweepy accesses on your device

Sweepy only works with data you grant it access to through iOS permissions. Each permission is optional; refusing it simply switches the related feature off.

2.1 Photo library

With Photos access, Sweepy reads your library to find what can be cleaned. It reads technical metadata (file name, type, size, dimensions, duration, creation date, Live Photo and burst flags, local identifiers) and analyses image content on the device using Apple's Vision and Core Image frameworks: visual similarity, duplicates, sharpness (blur), and, for photos that belong to a group, the number of faces, whether eyes are closed, and Apple's face-capture-quality score. This analysis is used only to group photos and to mark the one to keep. Sweepy does not identify people, does not create face templates that could recognise anyone, and never uploads photos, thumbnails or analysis results anywhere.

Photos stored only in iCloud are included when the "Check iCloud Photos" setting is on. In that case iOS downloads a small preview of each such photo to your device for analysis; this traffic goes between your iPhone and Apple's iCloud, not to us.

Deleting, compressing or converting items always requires your confirmation. Deleted photos and videos go to the Recently Deleted album in Apple Photos, where iOS keeps them for 30 days.

2.2 Contacts

With Contacts access, Sweepy reads names, phone numbers, email addresses, organisations and contact photos to find duplicates, similar names, entries without a name and entries without a number. Merging or deleting contacts happens only when you confirm it. Before any change, Sweepy saves a safety copy of the affected contacts inside the App on your device so you can restore them. Contact notes are read only if Apple has granted the App the Contacts Notes entitlement.

2.3 Device readings

For the Phone Health screen, Sweepy reads free and used storage, memory in use, thermal state, CPU load and battery level through public iOS APIs. A short history of free space is kept on the device to forecast when storage may run out. None of this is transmitted.

2.4 Notifications

If you allow notifications, Sweepy schedules local reminders (for example, when storage is almost full). These are generated on your device; no push service or notification token is used.

2.5 Email (Email Cleaner, when available)

Some versions of the App include an optional Email Cleaner for Gmail. If you connect a Google account, Sweepy talks to the Gmail API directly from your phone, with the permissions you grant on Google's consent screen. It reads message headers and sizes (sender, subject, date, labels), never message bodies or attachments, and moves messages you choose to Gmail's Trash. Your Google access tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain on your device; you can disconnect at any time in the App or at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Sweepy's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

3. What is stored, and where

Everything Sweepy keeps lives inside the App's own storage on your iPhone (and in your encrypted iCloud or iTunes device backup, if you make one):

  • scan results and the analysis cache described above;
  • your Basket, Keep List, Swipe decisions and dismissed contact groups;
  • contact safety copies (in the App's Application Support folder, protected by iOS file protection);
  • the Secret Library, if you use it: the photos and videos you move there are stored inside the App, and the PIN you choose is stored as a salted hash in the iOS Keychain. We cannot read or reset it;
  • settings, the storage history used for forecasts, and summary numbers shared with the Home and Lock Screen widgets through an App Group on the device;
  • for Email Cleaner: your Google tokens (Keychain) and a local list of senders, subjects, dates and sizes;
  • the random installation identifier and your analytics choice (section 4a), and, once Sweepy Pro exists, the subscription status received from Apple.

Deleting the App deletes all of it.

4. What leaves your device

Your photos, videos, contacts and emails never leave your device through Sweepy. Apart from the anonymous usage statistics described in section 4a, data leaves your phone only when you choose to send it yourself:

  • Sharing a photo, video or contacts backup goes through the iOS share sheet to the app or person you pick.
  • Emailing support or suggesting a feature opens your mail app with a message you can edit before sending. We then receive what you send: your email address and the message. We use it only to reply and to improve the App.
  • Rating the App uses Apple's in-app review prompt; the review goes to Apple.
  • Links to this website, Apple or Google open in your browser.
  • Email Cleaner talks to Google as described in section 2.5.

Sweepy contains no advertising or attribution SDKs and makes no network requests of its own apart from those listed here and in section 4a.

4a. Usage analytics

To understand which features are used and where the App fails, Sweepy uses Amplitude (Amplitude, Inc., USA), a product-analytics service. Analytics may be switched on in a given version of the App; when it is, the App sends Amplitude the following:

  • Product events — which screens are opened, which features are used, how many items were moved to the Basket, deleted, compressed or merged, and which permissions were granted. Events carry counts and categories only.
  • Technical context — App version, iOS version, device model, language, country derived from the IP address, and an installation identifier generated by the App (and, where available, Apple's vendor identifier, IDFV). The IP address is used by Amplitude to derive an approximate location and is not stored by us.

Analytics events never include photos, thumbnails, file names, contact names or numbers, email senders or subjects, the contents of the Secret Library, or anything else from Your Content. The installation identifier is random and is not tied to your name, Apple ID or email; we cannot use it to identify you. We do not use Amplitude for advertising, and we do not use Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) or request App Tracking Transparency permission.

You can turn analytics off at any time in the App under Settings › Share Usage Statistics; the switch applies immediately. Amplitude processes this data on our behalf under a data processing agreement and Amplitude's own security program; events are stored on Amplitude's servers in the United States (see section 11) and are deleted after 24 months.

When that happens, purchases will be processed by Apple through the App Store: Apple handles your payment details and we never see them. The App will receive from Apple a receipt and a transaction identifier confirming that a subscription is active, which it stores on your device and may send to Amplitude as a yes/no "subscriber" property and a product name, so that we can see which plans are popular. Subscription terms, prices, renewal and cancellation will be described in the App and in the Terms of Use before any paid feature goes live.

Usage analytics (section 4a) are processed on the basis of our legitimate interest in understanding how the App is used and in finding and fixing problems; where local law requires consent for analytics, we ask for it in the App before any event is sent, and you can withdraw it with the Settings switch. Photos, contacts and emails may incidentally contain special categories of data (for example, a photo of a medical document). Sweepy does not look for or interpret such content; it only compares images and reads contact fields to find duplicates.

8. Retention and deletion

  • On-device data is kept until you remove it in the App or delete the App.
  • The analysis cache is refreshed as your library changes and is removed with the App.
  • Contact safety copies: the App keeps the 30 most recent copies and removes older ones automatically.
  • Photos and videos you delete stay in Apple Photos › Recently Deleted for 30 days; Gmail messages you trash follow Google's Trash retention.
  • Analytics events are kept by Amplitude for 24 months and then deleted; aggregated statistics that no longer identify an installation may be kept longer.
  • Support correspondence is kept for up to 24 months after the last message, unless you ask us to delete it sooner or we need it to resolve a dispute.

9. Security

No system is perfectly secure; keep your device passcode on and your iOS up to date. If you believe the App has a security problem, please tell us at SUPPORT_EMAIL.
